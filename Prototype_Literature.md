## Initial Literature Review

**What prior work has been done in this area?**

Language shapes thought. The Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis, as we studied in our Cognitive Psychology course, states that language to some degree determines cognition. This foundational theory suggests that the structure of our language affects our worldview. We are applying this to the digital age: if our “Instagram language” changes after an election, does our “Instagram reality” change with it? 

Recent computational social science research involving natural language processing and comparative linguistics has further explored how language can be used to study human behavior. In the “From Text to Thought: How Analyzing Language Can Advance Psychological Science” study, researchers show that analyzing text data can reveal underlying psychological states and cognitive patterns. This supports our approach of using language as a proxy for belief and attitude changes.

Additional studies from 2024, “Highly engaging events reveal semantic and temporal compression in online community discourse,” show that major “shock” events do not just change what we talk about, but they entirely rewire the way we write. These findings suggest that large societal events can reshape how we comunicate therefore, reinforcing the importance of our plan to look at the “how” (language) to find the “what” (belief) is on the right track.
