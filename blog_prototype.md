## Blog Post Background Section
**Why is the problem important?**
Understanding how beliefs change over time is central to studying cultural and societal evolution. It is difficult to measure belief shifts directly, but language is an observable signal. Language reflects how individuals interpret events, express opinions, frame their opinions, etc. Here, we view analysis of language as a means of approximating changes in beliefs and discourse.

**The link between language and belief**
Language reflects and shapes how people think. The way individuals describe events reveals how they understand them. Changes in tone, framing, and word choice, among other linguistic features, can indicate shifts in attitudes and perspectives. We are not claiming direct belief change, but we treat language as a proxy for belief and discourse shifts. If systematic changes in language are observed across many individuals pre-election and post-election, this suggests broader cultural or cognitive shifts.

**Capturing cultural change through events**
Major societal events (e.g., presidential elections) can reshape public discourse. These events influence how people communicate, how they frame issues, and how they express opinions. By comparing pre-event and post-event language, we can test whether communication patterns change over time. If a model can distinguish between these periods, it suggests that the event produced measurable linguistic changes.

**Benefits of Instagram caption analysis**
Instagram captions are informal and real-world examples of language with both personal and public expression. They are less filtered than the news, and thus more authentic. They detail everyday reactions rather than curated narratives. Therefore, captions are an excellent means of collecting large-scale data for cultural trend analysis.

**Avoidance of the human blind spot**
Humans are naturally biased and often unable to see slow, structural changes as they happen. We are using Large Language Models like BERT because they can objectively scan thousands of captions to find patterns that are too subtle or too vast for a person to notice across many users. BERT allows for systematic detection of language changes.

**Broader implications**
Comparing politicians versus influencers helps identify where strong language shifts occur. Differences in model performance suggest differences in how strongly each group contributes to event-driven discourse.
If models detect consistent language shifts, they may provide early indicators of changes in discourse or sentiment. This could be useful for tracking social trends and studying polarization or public response.

**Main Takeaway:** If a model can reliably distinguish pre-event from post-event captions, this suggests that major societal events leave a measurable linguistic footprint in social media language, which may reflect broader shifts in discourse and potentially underlying beliefs.
